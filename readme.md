# [Fault Diagnosis] Optimize Envelope Spectrum through Candidate Fault Frequency Chart for Bearing Fault Diagnosis [[Attached MATLAB Code]]

Introduction Rolling bearings are precision mechanical components in modern industrial systems. Under adverse conditions, they are subjected to axial loads, radial loads, impact loads, and various external excitations, which can easily lead to structural fatigue cracks within the internal components. If not implemented with appropriate maintenance strategies in a timely manner, these can easily cause mechanical system failures, leading to considerable economic losses. Therefore, early detection of rolling bearing faults is crucial for ensuring the safety and reliability of mechanical systems [2,3].
The typical methods for fault detection in rolling bearings can be roughly considered as a cascade of filtering algorithms and envelope spectrum (ES) analysis. The filtering algorithm is used to enhance the repetitive transients or determine the frequency band containing diagnostic information observed in the bearing, while ES is used to identify the type and severity of the fault. Blind anti-convolution [4] is a typical filtering algorithm used to recover periodic or quasi-periodic pulses related to the fault from raw vibration signals, employing finite impulse response filters to eliminate the effects of various external signal transmission paths, and extracting features of repetitive transients related to the fault by maximizing a certain criterion. Based on metrics such as kurtosis, d-norm, multi-d-norm, correlation kurtosis, and cycle-stability indicators, blind anti-convolution algorithms including minimum entropy anti-convolution (MED), maximum correlation kurtosis anti-convolution (MCKD), and multi-point optimal minimum entropy anti-convolution adjustment (MOMEDA) are developed to enhance repetitive pulses related to the fault. Cheng et al. [8], [9], [10] proposed a new strategy for solving anti-convolution inverse filter based on statistical optimization algorithms and applied it to the extraction of weak fault characteristics in bearings. Antoni [11], [12], [13] introduced the spectral kurtogram (fast kurtogram, FK) as a pioneering filtering algorithm for determining information bands, where the vibration signal is divided into different bands using bandpass filtering; subsequently, the band with the highest kurtosis is selected for further analysis. Inspired by the FK algorithm, Protrugram [14], Autogram [15], Sparsogram [16], and average Inforgram [17] were proposed as various band selection algorithms to identify resonance frequency band faults in rolling bearings. In Protrugram, the kurtosis of the ES of the demodulated signal is considered the standard for determining the information band; compared to FK, this method is more robust to non-Gaussian noise. Unlike FK and Protrugram, Sparsogram/Inforgram measures the information level of fault detection based on the L2 and L1 norms (L2/L1 norms)/negentropy (NE) ratio of the square envelope of narrowband filtered signals. All these methods exhibit similar working mechanisms, namely, bandpass filter groups (e.g., wavelet packet filters [14,16] or 1/3 binary tree filters [13,17]) initially used to divide the vibration signal into different frequency bands, followed by selecting the best frequency band carrying the information related to the rolling bearing fault according to specified sparsity indicators. Additionally, similar band selection methods have also been developed and applied to bearing fault identification, which can be observed in literature [18], [19], [20].
Another effective alternative to ES-based bearing fault identification is Spectral Correlation (SC) / Spectral Ensemble Signal (SCoh), which is the normalized version of SC [21]. SC/SCoh is an important method for vibration signal analysis, revealing the second-order cyclic steady state of bearing faults by mapping the vibration signals onto a two-dimensional plane composed of spectral frequencies and cycle frequencies. The physical meaning of spectral frequencies is the same as that obtained through Fourier transform, while cycle frequencies contain repetitive transient pulse frequencies and their multipliers, which need to be identified in bearing fault diagnosis. However, SC/SCoh involves advanced stochastic process theory, requiring considerable computational time in some cases, significantly hindering its widespread application in quickly solving fault diagnosis problems. To address this issue, Antoni [22] proposed a fast and effective SC estimator, namely the Fast SC algorithm, which has appropriate convergence performance and low complexity. The Fast SC algorithm is based on short-time Fourier transform and uses Fast Fourier Transform to evaluate the scanned cyclic spectrum. Squared ES (SES) / Enhanced ES (EES) resulting from integrating SCoh over the entire frequency band of the full spectrum frequency can be used as an appropriate tool to extract bearing fault information, but it is sensitive to strong interference noise. Therefore, the spectrum generated by integrating SCoh over specific frequency bands with rich fault information, i.e., Improved ES (IES), can enhance the relevant cyclic components and perform fault detection. Wang et al. [23] proposed a guideline based on L2/L1 norms to determine the informational frequency bands with fixed bandwidth. This method scans the entire SCoh spectrum band using the frequency bands covering three times the competing fault frequencies within the bandwidth. Moreover, it selects the frequency band with the maximum L2/L1 norm as the best frequency band for generating diagnostic IES. Schmidt et al. [24] proposed an indicator based on signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) to determine the informational frequency bands for generating narrowband IES from SCoh. Mauricio et al. [25] proposed IESFOgram for IES optimization during non-stationary operation conditions for rolling bearing diagnosis. This method initially divides the spectrum band into a 1/3 binary tree structure and selects diagnostic features based on fault characteristic frequency (FCF) for generating IES. This method has been applied to the analysis of vibration signals in rotating machinery and achieved effective diagnostic performance under different conditions [26], [27], [28], [29]. Similarly, Chen et al. [30] developed a method based on FCF to determine the optimal frequency bands for bearing fault detection using SCoh and systematically compared the performance of various blind zone and target zone features under the IESFOgram framework. Moreover, Zhang et al. [31] proposed a strategy based on FCF by integrating SCoh over the full spectrum band with weighted integration to construct envelope spectrum tools. However, minor fluctuations around the nominal speed may damage the FCF-based method, even leading to poor fault detection capability. Therefore, there is a need for a more robust method that does not rely on sparseness indicators and FCF to adaptively guide the generation of IES for bearing fault detection from SCoh.
Therefore, this paper proposes an adaptive feature method IES via Candidate Fault Frequency-Optimization-gram (IESCFFOgram) to identify the information spectrum bands of SCoh for bearing fault diagnosis. In the new method, candidate fault frequency (CFF) is automatically identified based on the local features of SCoh instead of nominal FCF and further used to guide the selection of information bands. This new method completely eliminates the dependence on FCF or sparseness indicators, and can generate diagnostic IES by mining the fault information hidden in the SCoh plane. Therefore, the proposed IESCFFOgram is suitable for fault identification of rolling bearings under complex working conditions. The effectiveness and superiority of the proposed method are verified through simulation signals, vibration datasets obtained from artificially damaged bearing experiments, and accelerated bearing degradation tests.
